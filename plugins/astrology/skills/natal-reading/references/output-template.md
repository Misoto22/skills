# Natal reader report structure

Translate these headings semantically into the user's language. Keep the order.

## 1. Ascendant and the chart's shape

Open with how this chart tends to show up in a life, in plain language. Name the ascendant, its sign, and where its traditional ruler sits — that pairing is the spine of a classical reading. Then say what the chart's overall distribution looks like: clustered or spread, weighted above or below the horizon.

Say once that this is a traditional interpretive model, then write the interpretation without apologising through it.

## 2. The sect light and its condition

Sect decides which luminary leads. Name it, say why (Sun above or below the horizon), and read that light's sign, house, dignity and aspects as the chart's main thread.

This section is where dignities are first discussed, so state once that classical rulerships are used and modern assignments deliberately are not.

## 3. Tightest aspects, strengths and tensions

Take the three or four tightest aspects and develop them. For each: what the pairing is commonly read as, how it might show up day to day, and the countervailing condition that limits it.

Say when an aspect is wide. A 7° square belongs in a sentence, not a paragraph, and the difference is the reading's honesty about its own weight.

Flag any placement at a critical degree here, where the claim is made — not only in the data card.

## 4. Angles, lots, and reflection

The midheaven, descendant and imum coeli each carry a domain in this tradition; take the one or two the chart actually emphasises rather than walking all four. Add the lots that landed somewhere the rest of the chart already pointed.

Close with two or three low-risk reflection prompts — things the reader can watch for, not instructions to act on.

## 5. Model data card

Compact, late:

- ascendant sign and degree, sect light and its condition;
- house system and zodiac;
- no more than three further indicators;
- every recorded limitation, named;
- one sentence: traditional interpretive model, classical rulerships, not a measurement.

End with a single link to the evidence artifact.

## What not to do

Do not walk the bodies in order with a sentence each. Thirteen short paragraphs is a table wearing prose, and it hides which two or three placements the chart actually turns on.
