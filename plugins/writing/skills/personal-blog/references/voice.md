# Voice and evidence

A voice profile records observable writing choices for this task. It is not a
biography and does not authorize a personal claim.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest available basis, in this order:

1. The writer's explicit constraints and style directions.
2. The passage or draft being edited.
3. Representative samples supplied for this piece, ideally five to ten when available.
4. The writer's notes.
5. A neutral provisional voice when none of the above exists.

When sources conflict, follow explicit directions, preserve the current draft's
deliberate local choices, and use representative samples to resolve unstated
questions. Never infer identity, history, beliefs, relationships, preferences,
or emotions from stylistic patterns.

## Observable profile

Record only fields supported by the evidence. Leave the rest unspecified.

| Field | Observe |
|---|---|
| Point of view | First, second, or third person; distance from the subject. |
| Sentence rhythm | Length range, fragments, repetition, pauses, and variation. |
| Paragraph movement | How paragraphs turn, accumulate, contrast, or stop. |
| Diction | Concrete or abstract vocabulary, idiom, jargon, and compression. |
| Directness | How quickly claims and questions appear; degree of qualification. |
| Questions | Frequency and whether questions open inquiry or press a conclusion. |
| Transitions | Explicit signposts, abrupt cuts, echoes, or none. |
| Humour | Presence, dryness, targets, and frequency—never add it by default. |
| Code-switching | Languages mixed, where switches occur, and terms left untranslated. |
| Openings and endings | Characteristic entry, last-line length, closure, or openness. |

Preserve intentional roughness, ambiguity, repetition, fragments, and bilingual
phrasing when the evidence shows them. Do not normalize them merely to make the
prose more conventionally smooth.

For a line edit, mark the requested defect and the features the writer asked to
keep. Lock already-clear words, fragments, and sentence breaks that carry those
features; change only the span needed to fix the requested defect. Named
protected features outrank a general clarity request: a deliberate fragment is
locked, not a clarity defect. Before keeping any edit, identify the specific
ambiguity or likely misreading it fixes; if there is none, restore the original
span verbatim.

## Gaps stay visible

When a personal detail is structurally necessary but absent, either rewrite the
passage so it does not need that detail or insert a visible Markdown placeholder:

- `[Add the moment that changed your view here.]`
- `[Describe what you noticed on the train.]`
- `[Confirm the version and observed result.]`

Make the gap specific enough for the writer to fill. Do not hide it in prose and
do not convert a plausible inference into a memory or fact.

## AI-prose warning signs

Remove these unless the samples clearly support them:

- inflated stakes or fake universality;
- ceremonial transitions such as “In today's rapidly changing world”;
- symmetrical lists used only for rhythm;
- uniformly polished sentences that erase fragments or abrupt turns;
- unearned intimacy, autobiography, certainty, or emotion;
- a conclusion that paraphrases the opening instead of advancing or releasing it;
- a forced lesson, reassurance, or call to action.
