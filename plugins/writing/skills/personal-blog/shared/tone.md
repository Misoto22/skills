# Tone

The filler and fact-preservation rules below apply to all writing. The apology
and rewrite-ratio rules apply only when drafting or rewriting a message.

## When drafting or rewriting a message, use no unearned apology

An apology concedes fault. Attach one only where the sender is actually at fault. Lowering the temperature is not the same as conceding the point, and an unearned apology quietly transfers the advantage to the recipient.

Banned openers when nothing has gone wrong:

- Sorry to bother you
- Sorry for all the messages
- Apologies for chasing
- I hope this isn't a nuisance
- Just checking in — when it is in fact a third follow-up

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Sorry to bother you again, but did you get a chance to look at this? | Following up on this — I need an answer by Thursday to hold the date. |
| Apologies for chasing, I know you're swamped! | This has been open since Monday. Can you get to it this week? |

## When drafting or rewriting a message, avoid inflation

Keep the output within roughly 1.5× the input, unless a genuine deadline or consequence is being stated for the first time. Length past that is padding, and padding reads as evasion.

## In all writing, remove empty institutional filler

Delete on sight — these carry no information:

- I hope this email finds you well
- Thank you for your valuable time
- Just wanted to reach out
- I wanted to take a moment to
- Please don't hesitate to reach out
- As per my last email — if the point is to repeat something, repeat it

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| I hope this finds you well. I wanted to reach out and take a moment to follow up regarding the status of the invoice we discussed last week. | Following up on invoice #4471 — is it in this week's payment run? |

These read as evasive, and are the reason corporate writing is distrusted. Plain vocabulary is more professional, not less.

synergy · circle back · touch base · bandwidth · socialise an idea · double-click · align on · leverage as a verb · low-hanging fruit · boil the ocean · move the needle · at the end of the day · going forward · take this offline · ping, when it means chase

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Let's circle back on this once we have bandwidth. | I can't get to this until next week. |
| We should align on the approach before we double-click into the details. | Let's agree the approach before working through the details. |

## Preserve facts without inventing

Pass through unchanged: dates, times, ticket and version numbers, names, addresses, URLs, amounts, and quoted text.

Never invent a reason, date, or constraint. In non-message writing, add one only
when it is externally verifiable, backed by a verified source, and clearly
presented as sourced information rather than the writer's personal experience.

When drafting or rewriting a message, use only reasons, dates, and constraints
the writer supplied. Where the message needs one to function, insert a bracketed
placeholder — `[by Thursday]`, `[Module A]` — for the writer to complete. A
placeholder is honest; an invented deadline is not.
