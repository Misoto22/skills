# Cross-reading report structure

Translate these headings semantically into the user's language. Keep the order.
Machine detail belongs in the evidence artifact, not here.

## 1. What both charts describe

Open with the person's pattern in plain language — the thing both systems are
circling, before either system's vocabulary appears. Then say, in one sentence,
that this is two traditional models being compared with each other, not two
measurements converging on a fact.

Record the calendar-convention difference here when the year pillars differ:
BaZi turns the year at Li Chun, Zi Wei at lunar new year. State it as a
convention, not a discrepancy.

## 2. Where they agree

Develop two or three agreements properly. For each, give the BaZi evidence in
BaZi terms, the Zi Wei evidence in Zi Wei terms, and then what they jointly say
about the person's day-to-day.

Say when an agreement is not independent — two conclusions that both rest on the
birth hour are one piece of evidence wearing two hats. That sentence is worth
more than a third agreement.

## 3. Where they diverge

Never skip this section. Give each divergence its own short block:

- what each system says, in its own terms;
- whether this is 互补 (different facets) or 矛盾 (opposite answers to one question);
- what it does to the claim — a narrower statement, not a chosen winner.

If the charts genuinely agree everywhere, say what you checked that could have
disagreed and did not. An empty section three means the comparison stopped early.

## 4. What the divergence means for reading this

Turn section 3 into something usable. Which conclusions can be leaned on because
both systems support them, which should be held loosely, and which should be set
aside until the person's own experience settles them.

Close with two or three low-risk reflection prompts — things to watch for, not
instructions to act on.

## 5. Model data card

A small card, late:

- BaZi: day master, month command, five-element distribution;
- Zi Wei: life palace, bureau, the four transformations;
- the overall cross verdict — 同向 / 互补 / 矛盾 — as a qualitative label;
- one sentence stating that no combined score exists, because no defensible
  arithmetic merges a five-element distribution with a palace arrangement.

Do not print a merged number even if the reader asks for one. Explain why instead.

End with a single link to the evidence artifact.

## When alternates exist

Each system carries its own alternate. Add a short block before section 5 keeping
them apart: what both systems' primaries support, what each system's boundary
changes, and what should be withheld. Never pair one system's primary with the
other's alternate.
