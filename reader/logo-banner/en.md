`logo-banner` does not start by generating an image. It starts by settling the direction.

## The direction is agreed before anything is drawn

It reads the project first — the name, what it is for, who it is for, the visual language already in use, the constraints it has to respect — and then offers exactly three concrete directions, each stating a mood, a palette, a motif and a type character. You pick or amend one, and only then does generation begin.

Three is deliberate. One is no choice; five makes you compare near-identical options, and the usual outcome of that is starting over.

## One direction produces the whole set

The logo, the app icon, the favicon, the header image and the light and dark social banners all derive from that one agreed direction. That is why they look like a set rather than five separate drawings — assets generated independently drift apart in weight, spacing and motif even when the style description is the same.

## Without a generator, it stops

When no image generator is available it says so and stops rather than substituting another tool. An improvised logo gets used as though it were real, and the gap only surfaces later, in the one place that needs a vector source.

## What it does not do

It does not vectorise, does not write brand guidelines, does not do print layout, and does not generate anything before the direction is agreed.
