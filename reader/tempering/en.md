Off-the-shelf softening has one failure in common: removing the emotion removes the content with it. "Is anyone actually handling this" becomes "could you share the final conclusion when convenient" — grammatically professional, and the deadline, the consequence, and the reason the other person should prioritise it are all gone.

`tempering` works on one principle: remove the attack on the person, keep the pressure on the problem.

## Five fixed transformations

Five transformations do the work, and two of them carry most of it.

**An accusation becomes a dated request.** What "you never reply" is really carrying is how long this has been waiting and when it is needed, so the date has to survive the rewrite instead of becoming "as soon as possible".

**An open complaint becomes a closed question.** "What is going on with this" has no answer shape, and can be met with "we're looking into it". A question that can actually be answered gets a reply with something in it.

## Three registers, and you pick

The output is always three: collegial, on the record, and formally documented. Which one to send is the sender's judgement, because only you know how much goodwill the relationship has and how far this has already slipped.

Four situations are exceptions, where saying it plainly is the professional move: safety, compliance, a hard deadline already missed, and the third time of asking about the same thing. Softening there is the failure.

## It runs backwards too

Give it corporate phrasing and it says what that means. Whether "we'll revisit the prioritisation at an appropriate time" is "not this quarter" or "already cancelled" is a question you can ask it.

## What it does not do

It does not write marketing copy, resumes or blog posts, and does not do grammar cleanup with no interpersonal stakes. It sends nothing — sending is `email`.
